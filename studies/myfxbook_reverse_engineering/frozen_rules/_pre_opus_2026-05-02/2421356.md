## Family rationale

This system trades exclusively XAUUSD (1762/1763 trades) with entries concentrated in the 09:00-17:00 UTC window (London morning through NY-London overlap), peaking at 15:00-16:00 UTC. The exit kind is uniformly `manual_or_time`, and the direction split is near-balanced (52.1% Buy on XAUUSD). None of the standard FX-session families fits cleanly:

- `LATE_NY_BREAKOUT` requires 21:00-01:00 UTC peak — not present here.
- `LONDON_OPEN_MOMENTUM` / `LONDON_OPEN_MR` requires 06:09 UTC peak and FX majors — not present.
- `NY_SESSION_REVERSAL` requires entry 12-16 UTC with reversal logic against London move — the entry window partially overlaps but the pair is XAUUSD, not an FX cross.
- `OVERNIGHT_GAP_FADE` requires Friday-Monday weekend gap structure — not present.
- `MARTINGALE_GRID` was ruled out: k1_pass=PASS (martingale flag negative, steps=0).
- `OVERLAP_NY_LONDON_RANGE` is the closest geographic match (12-16 UTC, 233+223 trades at hours 15-16) but is defined for FX pairs using range-position logic. The fingerprint does not show a session-range gating pattern — instead, the dominant signal is a Bollinger Band position composite on H1 timeframe applied to Gold.

The best-fit family is therefore `FACTOR_SCALPING`: entries distributed across the trading day (not concentrated in a single 1-2 hour session window), driven by a composite of trend/momentum factors (BB position, EMA distance, multi-timeframe return), on a single liquid instrument (XAUUSD) traded on the M30 chart. Kaufman [trading_systems_methods, p.13] explicitly categorizes metals (gold) among "low-noise markets" that favor trend-following strategies, providing the economic rationale: Gold exhibits higher autocorrelation of returns at intraday horizons than equity indices, making momentum-based factor signals more reliable. Chan [algo_trading_chan, p.133] confirms that "time series momentum — past returns of a single instrument are positively correlated with future returns" is a distinct category from cross-sectional momentum and is observed in commodities.

The `FACTOR_SCALPING` assignment reflects that the dominant decision variable (bb_pos_20_2_H1, feature importance 0.73 in rank-1 tree) is a normalized position within a Bollinger Band — a standard "factor" measuring how far price has deviated from its rolling mean relative to recent volatility. This is a position-in-distribution signal, not a session-breakout signal. The secondary features (ema_dist_20_H1, ret_3_H1, ret_10_H4) reinforce the multi-factor composite character.
