# studies/lrs Phase 0 — Summary

> Curated narrative of phase-0 findings.
> Companion to the auto-generated [`report.md`](./report.md) (data view)
> and [`../../SPEC.md`](../../SPEC.md) (methodology). This file is written
> by hand and not regenerated by `run.py`.

## TL;DR

- **Tax-free leader**: **LRS-UPRO** (score `+0.1235`). The 200-day-SMA
  regime filter rotated into 3× S&P has a clear long-horizon edge in a
  frictionless world.
- **BR Lei 14.754 leader**: **B&H SSO** (score `+0.0313`). Annual tax on
  realized gains penalises rotation turnover hard enough that *holding*
  2× S&P beats *trading* it.
- **The 1y window is a whipsaw trap** — both LRS variants lose 60% of
  their 1-year windows. They only flip positive (tax-free) past the **5y
  window**. The deeper the horizon, the bigger the edge.
- **B&H UPRO (3× S&P held forever) is a trap** in all scenarios.
  Volatility decay + the 2008 99% drawdown leave its terminal multiple
  *below* B&H SSO (438× vs 690×), and its score is the worst of the five
  (`-0.1343`).
- This is discovery-only output (mandate §1). Nothing is deploy-ready;
  nothing is meant to be. Phase-0 establishes the scoring lens that
  every subsequent phase will use.

## What was tested

Five strategies on the modern-era window **1980-01-02 → 2026-05-21**
(11,692 trading bars, ~46 years):

| Strategy   | Holding rule                                    |
|------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| B&H SPY    | hold SPY forever (the universal benchmark)      |
| B&H SSO    | hold SSO (2× S&P) forever                       |
| B&H UPRO   | hold UPRO (3× S&P) forever                      |
| LRS-SSO    | SPY close > SMA200(SPY) → hold SSO; else cash@0% |
| LRS-UPRO   | same rule, holds UPRO when on                   |

Each strategy is scored **twice** in parallel:

- **`tax_free`** — the pre-tax pretend world.
- **`br_lei_14754`** — Brazil's Lei 14.754/2023 art. 5°/6°:
  15% annual on net realized gain, indefinite loss carry-forward.

Scoring follows the canonical lrs framework documented in [`../../SPEC.md`](../../SPEC.md):
overlapping rolling windows of {1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20} years (monthly step),
within-window composite `0.40·tanh(terminal_excess) + 0.25·time_above + 0.20·tanh(sortino) + 0.15·tanh(calmar)`,
per-length aggregation `0.60·mean + 0.40·p25`, across-length weights
`{1y: 5%, 3y: 10%, 5y: 15%, 10y: 20%, 15y: 25%, 20y: 25%}`. B&H SPY is
the universal benchmark for every strategy.

## Headline results

```
                tax-free      BR Lei 14.754   Δ tax cost
B&H SPY        +0.0000         +0.0000          0.0000   (anchor, by construction)
B&H SSO        +0.0313         +0.0313          0.0000   (no realized gains during window)
B&H UPRO       -0.1343         -0.1343          0.0000
LRS-SSO        +0.0819         -0.0632         -0.1451
LRS-UPRO       +0.1235         +0.0204         -0.1031
```

Two different worlds, two different verdicts:

- In **`tax_free`**, the ranking is `LRS-UPRO > LRS-SSO > B&H SSO > B&H SPY > B&H UPRO`.
- In **`br_lei_14754`**, the ranking is `B&H SSO > LRS-UPRO > B&H SPY > LRS-SSO > B&H UPRO`.

The flip from "LRS-UPRO leads by `+0.092` over B&H SSO" to "B&H SSO leads
by `+0.011` over LRS-UPRO" is the central economic finding of phase-0.
It is not a result about the *signal* — Gayed's SMA200 cross is real edge.
It is a result about the *friction*: annual realization tax under Lei
14.754 chews `~0.10–0.15` units of score off any high-turnover strategy.

### What this says

1. **The 1y window is dominated by whipsaws.** SMA200 cross can produce
   3–6 false breaks per year. Inside any given 12-month window, the
   strategy spends meaningful time in cash during a recovering market or
   re-enters at unfavourable prices. 1y win rate `~40%` is honest evidence
   that short-horizon evaluation is the wrong lens for this signal.

2. **The 5y window is the inflection.** Tax-free, LRS-SSO and LRS-UPRO
   both cross the 50% win-rate line around 5y (60% and 65% respectively).
   Below that, the signal is noise; above it, it's edge.

3. **10y and beyond is where the edge becomes overwhelming.** Tax-free,
   LRS variants beat SPY in **75-84%** of 10-20y windows. This is the
   signal Gayed (2016/2020) measured in the original paper, reproduced
   here on the modern-era window.

4. **Tax shifts the inflection point right.** Under Lei 14.754, LRS-SSO
   never reliably wins (50% at 5y, 59% peak at 10y, then drifts down to
   57% at 20y as cumulative tax drag compounds). LRS-UPRO still wins —
   70-72% at 10-20y — but with the score knocked down by half.

5. **p25 tells the real story for risk-aware investors.** The mean
   length_score for LRS-SSO at 15y is `+0.054` under tax — looks
   acceptable. The **p25 is `-0.125`** — one in four 15-year holding
   windows would have been materially below SPY. That kind of regime
   risk doesn't show up in full-period CAGR but does show up in the
   per-length aggregate.

## Tax friction analysis

The `br_lei_14754` scenario is the same strategies, same data, same
signal — only the tax model differs:

- **B&H curves never close a lot during the window**, so they realize no
  gain and pay no tax. Their tax-free and taxed scores are identical
  (Δ = 0). This is faithful to a held-forever BR investor.
- **LRS curves trigger ~265 switches over 46 years** (≈5.7/year, in line
  with SMA200's typical false-break rate). Each `ON→OFF` close realizes
  a gain or loss; the year's net positive gain is taxed at 15% on
  Dec 31, with losses carrying forward indefinitely under art. 6°.
- **Tax events**: 39 for LRS-SSO, 40 for LRS-UPRO. Essentially every
  calendar year had at least one closure with a net gain — the carry-
  forward rarely got to accumulate.
- **Cumulative tax drag**: `43.2` equity units for LRS-SSO, `118.6` for
  LRS-UPRO over the full window. That's the gross sum of all annual
  debits; the *compounded* terminal impact is larger because each debit
  forgoes future growth.

Translating to terminal multiples:

| Strategy  | Tax-free terminal | Taxed terminal | "Tax tax" (multiples lost) |
|-----------|------------------:|---------------:|---------------------------:|
| LRS-SSO   |          597.8×   |       321.0×   |   276.8× (46% destroyed)   |
| LRS-UPRO  |        1,835.4×   |       955.6×   |   879.8× (48% destroyed)   |

Annual tax destroys roughly **half the terminal wealth** of an LRS
strategy over 46 years — even though the per-year rate is "only" 15%.
This is why B&H is so competitive in BR: deferring realization is
itself a return.

A useful framing: in a frictionless world, the SMA200 rotation buys you
a `+0.10` to `+0.12` score (vs B&H SSO/UPRO). The BR tax model takes
back `0.10` to `0.15` of that. The signal is real; the tax is, on this
calibration, almost exactly equal to the signal.

## Per-strategy reading

### B&H SPY — the anchor (`0.0000` in all scenarios)

By construction. Scored against itself it produces `terminal_excess = 0`,
`time_above = 0.5` (winning-fraction convention treats ties as half),
`sortino_excess = 0`, `calmar_excess = 0`. Every window contributes
exactly 0. Useful as a sanity check; in the real workflow, B&H SPY is
the benchmark, not a strategy.

Full-window stats over 1980-2026: terminal 217.2×, CAGR 12.30%, MDD 55.14%
(2008-2009), Sortino 1.04.

### B&H SSO — the surprise winner under tax (`+0.0313` both scenarios)

Holding 2× S&P forever produces terminal 690.0× over 46 years (vs 217×
for SPY) at the cost of an 88.27% drawdown in 2008. The score is positive
but small: terminal_excess strongly positive, time_above moderate,
sortino *negative* (`0.81 vs 1.04`), calmar negative. The terminal win
carries most of the weight; the risk-adjusted components partly offset.

Under Lei 14.754 the score is **unchanged** because B&H realizes no
gain during the window. This is the structural reason it wins the
tax-world ranking: it pays zero tax through the entire test.

The realism caveat: a real BR investor would face a terminal-sale tax
on exit, which we don't model (it would apply to every strategy and
not change ranks). Also, a 46-year holding period with an 88% drawdown
is psychologically a fantasy for most retail investors.

### B&H UPRO — the leverage trap (`-0.1343` both scenarios)

3× S&P held forever has terminal **438.0×** — *less* than 2× B&H SSO
(690×). Volatility decay (the daily-rebalanced LETF formula
`r = L·r_SPX − fee/252` is convex in the wrong direction for high L)
plus the 98.31% drawdown in 2008-2009 destroyed the leverage premium.
Sortino 0.73 is the worst of the five strategies. The score is
unambiguously negative.

This is the cleanest single-strategy result of phase-0: **more leverage
is not more return**. Buy-and-hold 3× has been worse than buy-and-hold
2× on the modern-era window, contradicting naive expectations and
matching the Gayed paper's pre-paper-era simulation.

### LRS-SSO — the strategy that tax breaks (`+0.0819` → `-0.0632`)

Tax-free, this is a respectable winner: `+0.082` score, terminal
597.8×, MDD 55.02% (almost identical to SPY!), Sortino 0.97. The
rotation does exactly what it's supposed to do: capture most of the
upside of holding 2× S&P while exiting before the worst drawdown.

Under tax, the score flips to `-0.063` — *worse than B&H SPY*. Why?
The tax compounds. Terminal drops from 597.8× to 321.0×. The signal
still works at 10y+ horizons (59% win rate, length_score `-0.034`,
nearly break-even) but the cumulative drag turns the overall score
negative.

**Reading for BR investors**: in this calibration LRS-SSO is not worth
the operational complexity. B&H SSO has the same expected exposure,
zero turnover, zero annual tax events, and a higher tax-world score.

### LRS-UPRO — the strategy that survives tax (barely) (`+0.1235` → `+0.0204`)

Tax-free, LRS-UPRO is the winner: `+0.124` score, terminal **1835.4×**,
MDD 73.20% (much better than B&H UPRO's 98.31%), Sortino 0.88. The
3× exposure inside a regime filter behaves the way naive intuition
suggests 3× *should* behave: enormous upside, manageable downside.

Under tax, terminal drops to 955.6×, Sortino to 0.82, MDD essentially
unchanged at 73.68%. The score drops to `+0.0204` — still positive,
still positive at 10/15/20y windows (length_scores `+0.062`, `+0.080`,
`+0.081`), but **smaller than B&H SSO's `+0.0313`** by a hair. In the
tax world, LRS-UPRO is competitive with but does not clearly beat
naive 2× buy-and-hold.

Observations from this table that don't directly feed the score:

- **Switches per year**: 265 / 46 ≈ 5.7. Consistent with SMA200's
  historical false-break density on the S&P 500.
- **Tax-event count**: 39-40 over 46 years ≈ ~0.87/year. Most years had
  *at least one* closure that ended in net positive gain; the loss
  carry-forward only kicked in in a handful of years.
- **MDD of LRS-SSO is identical to SPY** (55.02% vs 55.14%). The rotation
  does exactly what Gayed claimed: 2× exposure on the way up, cash on the
  way down. The drawdown profile is essentially unlevered.
- **MDD of LRS-UPRO (73.20%) is far better than B&H UPRO (98.31%)** but
  still worse than B&H SPY. The 3× exposure during regime transitions
  bleeds enough that you can't get the 2x MDD profile from a 3x LETF
  even with a perfect signal.

## Sanity checks performed in `run.py`

All five passed cleanly on the final run:

1. **B&H SPY self-score == 0** in both scenarios. Catches a sign bug in
   the composite formula. (Caught one during development: the original
   `(ratio > 1.0)` formula gave a `-0.25` self-score because ties never
   strictly exceed 1; switched to winning-fraction convention with
   ties = 0.5.)
2. **B&H curves: tax-free score == taxed score.** Realises nothing during
   the window, so the two scenarios are mathematically identical.
3. **Tax can never improve a score.** LRS-SSO taxed (`-0.063`) < LRS-SSO
   tax-free (`+0.082`); LRS-UPRO taxed (`+0.020`) < LRS-UPRO tax-free
   (`+0.124`). Catches tax-application bugs.
4. **Total tax drag is non-negative and finite.** Trivial but easy to
   break with an off-by-one in the year-end settlement.
5. **Window counts in expected range.** Monthly step over 46 years
   yields ~525 1y windows down to ~315 20y windows, matching the
   tolerance bands in the sanity check.

## Caveats

- **Synthetic pre-2006/2009 SSO/UPRO bars.** testfol.io's leveraged
  series before the real ETF inceptions are modelled via Gayed's
  `r = L·r_SPX − fee/252`, not measured. Phase 2+ will add a Tiingo
  real-ETF overlay on the post-inception window for OOS sanity.
- **No frictions beyond tax.** Phase 0 has zero commission, zero spread,
  zero slippage, zero cash yield on the off-leg. A whipsaw-heavy signal
  will look better here than in production. Phase 1 will layer in Inter
  Internacional commission, ~5 bps spread per switch and CASHX yield.
- **FX gain on USD/BRL not modelled.** A real BR investor pays IR on
  currency appreciation against the real. We assume strategy ranks are
  preserved because every strategy sees the same FX series — true for
  comparing within phase-0 but a real-world drag against all of them.
- **Single-window descriptive run.** No walk-forward, no CPCV, no PBO,
  no DSR. Phase 0 cannot make a robustness claim. The scoring framework
  is set up to feed into those panels in later phases.
- **Sortino convention.** This codebase's Sortino uses
  `√(mean(min(r, 0)²))` (Estrada-style). Sortino values reported here
  are not directly comparable with conventions that use
  `√(mean(neg_returns²) / n_neg)`. Used consistently for strategy and
  benchmark within each window, so the *excess* is well-defined.
- **The "best" depends on the lens.** Tax-free says LRS-UPRO; tax-aware
  says B&H SSO. The right answer for a specific investor depends on
  their tax regime, holding-period preference, drawdown tolerance and
  operational appetite for monthly rebalances.

## Implications for phase 1+

Several concrete handoffs:

1. **Friction-stress LRS-SSO first.** It looks fragile under tax;
   commission and spread will only make it worse. If realistic
   frictions tip it solidly negative, drop it from the universe — the
   regime filter doesn't pay for 2× leverage in BR.
2. **LRS-UPRO is the candidate worth defending.** It's the only LRS
   variant that beats SPY tax-aware at 10y+ horizons. Phase 1 should
   stress it with commission/spread, CASHX yield, and an explicit
   walk-forward of the SMA window {50, 100, 125, 150, 200} per
   `[leverage_for_the_long_run, p.14, Table 6]`.
3. **B&H SSO is the de facto benchmark for any future leveraged
   rotation.** Beating B&H SPY isn't enough — beating taxed B&H SSO is
   the actual bar a deployable strategy must clear.
4. **Per-window p25, not just mean, is the real verdict.** A length_score
   of `+0.080` with a `+0.078` p25 (LRS-SSO 15y tax-free) is much more
   robust than a length_score of `+0.080` with a `-0.125` p25 (LRS-SSO
   15y taxed). The framework already exposes p25 in the report; future
   phases should make it the primary lens, not the mean.
5. **The 5y window is the credibility threshold.** Anything below it is
   noise dominated; anything above it carries signal. Phase 1 sweeps
   should at minimum report 5/10/15/20y per-length scores.
